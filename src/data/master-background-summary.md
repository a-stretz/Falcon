## Most Relevant Recent Experience

### AI and Computer Vision Platform (Performance Analysis)

I worked on a platform that uses computer vision and machine learning to capture and analyze human movement in real time. My role was product — not the model, not the infrastructure, not the data pipeline — but the product layer that made the system useful and trustworthy to end users.

**Key areas of ownership:**

- **User-facing analysis interfaces:** Defined what model outputs should surface to users, how thresholds and interpretation logic should work, and what constitutes meaningful signal vs. noise. This includes what I think of as the SmartPlayer work — turning raw computational outputs into understandable, interpretable product experiences.

- **Feedback system product definition:** Owned the requirements and logic for a system that converted model outputs into contextual coaching language. This included defining coverage, verbosity, specificity, result framing, encouragement logic, tone calibration, and persona variants for different user contexts. The system needed to feel like a knowledgeable coach, not a database query.

- **QA and diagnostic workflows:** Designed and led QA processes for validating model output quality. Built test case libraries, edge case documentation, and evaluation criteria. Established when outputs should be flagged, suppressed, or surfaced with caveats. This work was about building internal trust in what the system produced before it went to customers.

- **Onsite capture and calibration workflows:** Owned the operational workflow design for physical camera setup and system calibration at customer sites. This was end-to-end workflow PM work — from operator arrival through validated data capture — and directly impacted data quality entering the model pipeline.

- **Structured product documentation:** Introduced and owned PRDs, user stories, acceptance criteria, roadmap artifacts, and documentation standards. Brought structured product definition to an environment that had been operating primarily on informal technical knowledge.

- **Cross-functional alignment:** Regularly translated between ML team thinking and business/stakeholder language. Owned alignment on interpretation standards and display criteria when different teams had conflicting mental models of what the model output meant.
