# Sample Scoring Rubric

## Evaluation Dimensions (1–5 scale)

### 1. Relevance to the Question
Does the answer actually address what was asked?
- 1: Off-topic or answers a different question entirely
- 2: Partially relevant but misses the core ask
- 3: Addresses the question but doesn't fully engage with it
- 4: Directly and fully addresses the question
- 5: Addresses the question with precision and shows awareness of what the interviewer is really probing for

### 2. Structure and Clarity
Is the answer well-organized and easy to follow?
- 1: Disorganized, hard to follow, no discernible structure
- 2: Loosely structured with significant gaps or jumps
- 3: Reasonably structured but with some meandering or unclear transitions
- 4: Clear structure throughout — easy to track the narrative
- 5: Exceptionally clear, logical flow that makes the answer feel practiced and credible

### 3. Specificity
Does the answer use concrete details, or does it stay vague and generic?
- 1: Entirely generic — could apply to any PM at any company
- 2: Mostly generic with one or two real details
- 3: Mix of specific and vague — needs more grounding
- 4: Mostly specific with real context, decisions, and details
- 5: Highly specific — names the thing, the decision, the tradeoff, the outcome

### 4. Product Judgment
Does the answer show genuine PM thinking — prioritization, tradeoffs, user insight, decisions?
- 1: No evidence of product thinking
- 2: Surface-level PM language without real reasoning
- 3: Some product judgment present but not developed
- 4: Clear product thinking — shows real decision-making
- 5: Strong, nuanced product judgment — demonstrates ownership and thoughtful reasoning

### 5. Technical Fluency (PM-appropriate)
Does the candidate demonstrate appropriate technical literacy without overstepping?
- 1: Either avoids all technical content or overclaims technical ownership
- 2: Technical language used incorrectly or without understanding
- 3: Technically adequate — doesn't embarrass but doesn't impress
- 4: Shows genuine technical understanding appropriate to a PM
- 5: Clearly technically literate — can discuss model behavior, data quality, system tradeoffs at PM depth

### 6. Business Framing
Does the answer connect to business value, user impact, or strategic context?
- 1: No business connection
- 2: Business context mentioned but superficially
- 3: Some business framing but underdeveloped
- 4: Clearly connects decisions to business or user value
- 5: Strong business framing throughout — decisions are clearly tied to outcomes that matter

### 7. Metrics and Outcomes
Does the answer include measurable results or concrete evidence of impact?
- 1: No metrics or outcomes whatsoever
- 2: Vague outcome language ("it went well," "users liked it")
- 3: Some outcome language but not quantified or grounded
- 4: Clear outcome with directional metrics or credible evidence
- 5: Strong outcome language with specific metrics, before/after framing, or verifiable proof points

### 8. Conciseness
Is the answer appropriately concise, or does it ramble?
- 1: Very long with significant padding, repetition, or tangents
- 2: Too long — needs significant trimming
- 3: Roughly the right length but with excess that could be cut
- 4: Well-calibrated — gets the job done without wasting the interviewer's time
- 5: Tight and efficient — every sentence earns its place

### 9. Credibility
Does the answer sound like someone who has actually done this work?
- 1: Generic, textbook-sounding, or implausible
- 2: Partially credible but with gaps that create doubt
- 3: Generally credible with some weak spots
- 4: Credible — the interviewer would believe this person has done this
- 5: Highly credible — specific, grounded, and consistent with the rest of what's known about the candidate

---

## Recurring Weakness Categories

These should be tracked across sessions to identify patterns:

| Tag | Description |
|-----|-------------|
| `rambling` | Answer is too long and loses focus |
| `vague-outcome` | Result described in vague or generic terms |
| `missing-metrics` | Impact not quantified even when metrics likely exist |
| `weak-structure` | Answer is hard to follow or disorganized |
| `missing-decision-logic` | Actions described without explaining WHY |
| `poor-prioritization-logic` | Prioritization answer lacks defensible criteria |
| `no-business-framing` | Answer stays at feature/execution level without business connection |
| `overuse-of-same-story` | Relying too heavily on one example across questions |
| `too-technical` | Candidate slips into engineering mode |
| `not-technical-enough` | Candidate avoids technical content inappropriately |
| `missing-ownership` | Answer is vague about what the candidate personally owned |
| `generic-language` | Uses PM clichés instead of real language |
| `weak-opener` | Answer takes too long to establish the point |
| `no-clear-result` | Story ends without a clear outcome |

## Overall Score Interpretation

| Range | Label | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| 4.5–5.0 | Strong | Ready to use — maybe minor polish |
| 3.5–4.4 | Solid | Good foundation, clear improvement areas |
| 2.5–3.4 | Developing | Some useful content but significant gaps |
| 1.5–2.4 | Weak | Needs significant rework |
| 1.0–1.4 | Not Ready | Start over or skip this question |
